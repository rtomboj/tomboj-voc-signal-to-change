# Decision and delivery

Use this worksheet to make a company decision from feedback. The proposed roles and cadence are Tomboj design choices. Reuse the company's existing decision forum where it works.

## Select the level of change

| Fix type | Example | Likely accountable role | Delivery check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Individual coaching | A knowledge gap in explaining a process | Supervisor | Observed behavior and customer outcome; no score-based ranking |
| Team process | An unreliable handoff between shifts | Team lead | Handoff completion and repeat-contact rate |
| Cross-functional process/policy | Conflicting refund rules across teams | Named process owner with decision authority | End-to-end completion, time, rework, and exceptions |
| Product/technology | A defect or unmet user need | Product/engineering owner | Validated task outcome, adoption, recurrence, and regressions |
| Department | Capacity, incentives, or operating model | Department leader | Service outcome, staff workload, and cost/quality tradeoffs |
| Organization/strategy | Pricing, service model, structure, or market commitment | Executive sponsor and accountable delivery owner | Strategic outcome and customer/employee impact over the agreed window |

Classify by the actual decision needed, not the job title mentioned in a review. Several levels may contribute. Put local remediation in Step 3 and link it to the larger initiative where necessary.

## Intake and root cause

Accept opportunities from customer cases, repeated monitored signals, frontline learning, operational data, and positive practices worth expanding. Marketplace context may suggest an investigation but is not direct evidence of this company's customer experience.

For each opportunity, capture the problem and affected journey, evidence window/coverage, source and case links, independent report count, known impact, root-cause hypothesis, counterevidence, present workaround, and requested decision. A count from an accessible public sample is not an estimate of all customers affected. Flag evidence gaps without forcing every severe issue to wait for more reports.

Use a short causal investigation: compare cases, check the actual process or product, ask where the failure enters, and test alternative explanations. “Train the employee” is not an adequate root cause when permissions, incentives, tooling, or staffing prevent the right action. Keep people investigations restricted and separate from the general improvement register.

## Prioritization worksheet

Use a comparative table; do not invent a precise ROI or a weighted score when inputs are unknown.

| Criterion | Question | Evidence to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Severity and customer impact | How much does the outcome matter, and to whom? | Observed impact versus estimated reach; affected audience |
| Evidence strength | How confident are we in the problem and cause? | Independent sources, operational confirmation, contrary evidence |
| Strategic fit | Does this support a committed business/customer goal? | Explicit goal and tradeoff |
| Effort and dependencies | What must change, and who must participate? | Range estimate, systems/teams, unknowns |
| Readiness | Can the organization adopt and maintain the change? | Capacity, training, ownership, dependency readiness |
| Time to benefit | When could an outcome be observed? | Milestones and measurement window |
| Positive opportunity | What valuable behavior or experience should be preserved or expanded? | Evidence of value and risks of losing it |

If the user wants a score, define scales and weights with them, show underlying evidence, and include a sensitivity check. Do not multiply confidence into zero priority for a potentially severe event; urgent investigation is a distinct decision.

## Decision rights and cadence

Propose weekly intake/triage, a monthly cross-functional decision forum, and milestone reviews for accepted work. Existing urgent escalation takes precedence; never wait for the monthly forum to route serious harm. Scale the cadence to volume and capacity.

- The intake coordinator maintains the evidence and decisions; they need not own delivery.
- The decision-maker accepts/investigates/defers/declines/merges with a reason and date.
- The change owner accepts accountability for delivery and updates; contributors own specific tasks.
- The customer communication owner retains customer commitments and translates decisions into relevant updates.
- The verification reviewer checks evidence against pre-agreed criteria.

Draw a capacity boundary: accepted work needs an owner, authority, resources, and next milestone. Do not call an unfunded wish list an approved roadmap. For deferred work, specify a review date or objective revisit condition plus owner. For declined work, preserve the reason and explain it to the originating team/customer where appropriate.

## Delivery and adoption

Create a small pilot with a measurable baseline before broad rollout. Specify success and adverse-effect criteria in advance. List dependencies, decision checkpoints, accountable roles, rollback/stop conditions, and support for affected staff. Do not treat a training session as evidence that behavior changed.

Continue customer-level workarounds while larger work progresses. Communicate changed dates before a commitment is missed where possible. Preserve decision history when scope changes; record what was stopped, why, and what replaces it. Update Step 2 measurement/configuration only through its authorized change/test process, preserving the historical baseline and documenting taxonomy changes.
