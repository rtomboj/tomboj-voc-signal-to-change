# Conversation and coaching

Use these exercises as a proposed enablement plan. They are not a validated certification or a mandatory script. Combine judgment with access to information and authority to act.

## Conversation sequence

1. **Prepare:** read relevant history, prior promises, known impact, and the permitted channel. Separate facts from allegations. Identify what can be offered now and what needs approval.
2. **Open specifically:** acknowledge the actual experience and ask whether this is a suitable time/channel. Avoid generic apologies that conceal what happened.
3. **Understand:** ask about impact, desired outcome, and anything still blocked. Reflect back the specifics; invite correction. Do not debate a rating or ask the customer to change a review.
4. **Diagnose:** confirm what is known, what needs checking, and who can investigate. Do not assign blame, defend policy reflexively, or make the customer repeat available information.
5. **Agree next steps:** state the action, responsible person, timing, next update, and how to reach the owner. Distinguish an investigation commitment from a promise to fix or build something.
6. **Verify:** after delivery, ask whether the intended outcome now works and whether anything remains unresolved. Log a customer statement or observed result; silence is not agreement.

Apologize honestly where appropriate and thank the customer for useful detail. Do not apply “always say thank you instead of sorry” as a rule. The fair remedy, effort required, and treatment all matter. Service recovery does not guarantee greater loyalty than avoiding the failure.

## Draft message patterns

Adapt to known facts, channel, and voice. Brackets require confirmed information. Keep drafts marked **Draft — not sent**.

**First contact:** “Thank you for telling us about [specific experience]. I understand [known impact]. I will coordinate the follow-up. Could you confirm [one necessary clarification]? We will [authorized next action], and I will update you by [date/time/timezone], even if the investigation is still underway.”

**Progress/delay:** “We have [verified progress]. [Remaining question] is still being checked by [team/role]. [Approved workaround] is available now. Our next update is [date/time]; we do not yet have a confirmed completion date.”

**Not planned:** “We reviewed [request]. We are not planning [change] because [clear, approved reason]. [Alternative/workaround, if real] may help with [outcome]. [Revisit commitment only if approved].”

**Verification:** “We completed [specific action] on [date]. Has this addressed [original outcome], and is anything still preventing you from [goal]?”

For public responses, acknowledge the experience without exposing account details; offer an approved private support route. Do not imply that an unknown reviewer has already received a remedy.

## Enablement plan

| Capability | Practice | Observable readiness evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context and evidence | Prepare a case with conflicting notes | Separates customer report, confirmed fact, and hypothesis |
| Listening and de-escalation | Role-play a frustrated customer and a constructive promoter | Restates specific impact, asks open questions, avoids defensiveness |
| Remedy judgment | Choose an action with an unknown approval limit | Uses allowed remedies; escalates instead of inventing authority |
| Expectation setting | Explain a delay or a declined request | Gives a truthful reason, owner, and update date without a false promise |
| Accurate capture | Independently classify the same five synthetic cases | Explains differences, preserves source IDs, marks uncertainty |
| Coordination | Hand off to an unavailable specialist | Maintains a communication owner and checks acceptance |
| Closure | Customer is silent after a workaround | Records unconfirmed outcome and an appropriate follow-up/disposition |

Suggested pilot: a 60-minute orientation, three role-plays, two supervised real cases where authorized, then a supervisor review against the behaviors above. Adapt to staff experience and case complexity. These durations and sample counts are design choices, not research benchmarks.

## Ongoing coaching

Run a short weekly learning huddle during the pilot; shorten or increase cadence if volume warrants it. Review one effective conversation, one difficult case, one overdue commitment, and one possible systemic obstacle. End with an owner and check-back date for each experiment.

Use a simple rubric of **Observed / Needs practice / Not observed** for preparation, listening, accuracy, judgment, expectation setting, documentation, and follow-through. Sample different channels and outcomes, including apparently successful cases; do not select only low scores. A score change alone cannot establish whether coaching worked.

Have peers or supervisors compare classifications periodically and explain disagreements. Preserve versioned taxonomy changes. Review actual action outcomes after practice. If scripts, tools, staffing, or policy block good service, route the obstacle for company improvement instead of treating it as an individual attitude problem.

Use recordings only with the company's applicable permissions and access controls. Keep sensitive material out of open huddles. Support staff after abusive or draining interactions and use the existing escalation policy. Never create employee league tables from customer comments.
